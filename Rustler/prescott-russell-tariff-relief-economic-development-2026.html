Prescott-Russell wants its share of tariff-relief money

Regional economic officials say agriculture, agri-food, manufacturing and transportation are exposed to trade disruption and need a stronger diversification plan.

By The Rustler staff · Published September 5, 2026 · Updated 4:25 p.m.

Prescott-Russell’s economic-development organization says it intends to pursue federal assistance for businesses and communities hurt by U.S. tariffs.

Prescott-Russell Community Development Corporation executive director John Candie told United Counties council in late August that his organization is working to ensure the region receives whatever assistance it qualifies for.

The discussion formed part of a broader review of the region’s economic-development strategy.

Prescott-Russell’s economy has particular exposure in agriculture, agri-food, manufacturing and transportation, industries that often depend on cross-border markets and supply chains. The PRCDC has also launched a strategic-positioning project aimed at helping local companies adapt to U.S. trade disruption, diversify markets and attract new investment.

Regional officials are looking at what should come next. Among the ideas receiving attention are increased support for agriculture and agribusiness, stronger destination and regional marketing, and attempts to capitalize on Prescott-Russell’s position between Ottawa, Montreal and the Mirabel aerospace cluster.

The local test will be how much of that work eventually reaches individual farmers, small businesses and employers in communities such as Russell and Embrun.
