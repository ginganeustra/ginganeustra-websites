First human West Nile case of the year confirmed in Eastern Ontario

The health unit says the case confirms infected mosquitoes are circulating and can remain active well into the fall.

By The Rustler staff · Published September 5, 2026 · Updated 4:25 p.m.

The Eastern Ontario Health Unit has confirmed the region’s first human case of West Nile virus in 2026, shortly after a bird in the health unit’s territory tested positive for the virus.

Dr. Paul Roumeliotis, the region’s medical officer of health, said the finding shows that some mosquitoes in the region are infected and transmitting the virus to humans and animals.

For most people, the risk of serious illness from West Nile virus is low. In some people, however, the virus can cause significant illness, including neurological complications.

The health unit is reminding residents not to assume mosquito season is over simply because September has arrived. Mosquitoes can remain active well into the fall.

People spending time outdoors can reduce their risk by using an approved insect repellent, wearing long sleeves and long pants when mosquitoes are active, and taking particular care around dusk and dawn.

Around the home, residents can reduce breeding areas by dumping standing water from wheelbarrows, tires, containers and children’s toys, changing bird-bath water regularly, clearing drains and eavestroughs, and maintaining screens on doors and windows.

The precise location and identity of the person infected have not been made public.
