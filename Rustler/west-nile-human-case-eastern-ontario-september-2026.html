West Nile warning remains in force across Eastern Ontario

A positive bird test shows the virus remains present, and the health unit is urging residents to keep taking precautions into the fall.

By The Rustler staff · September 5, 2026 · Updated 7:00 p.m.

The Eastern Ontario Health Unit is reminding residents that West Nile virus remains present in the region after a bird tested positive for the virus.

The finding means the risk does not disappear with the end of August. Mosquitoes can stay active into the fall, particularly while temperatures remain mild.

“This finding shows that West Nile virus remains a concern in our area as long as mosquitoes are active,” Medical Officer of Health Dr. Paul Roumeliotis said.

For most people, the risk of serious illness is low, but West Nile can cause significant illness and neurological complications in some people.

EOHU recommends using approved insect repellent, wearing long sleeves and long pants when mosquitoes are active, repairing window and door screens and reducing standing water around homes.

Local reports published this week conflict on whether a human case has also been confirmed in 2026. One report said EOHU had confirmed a first human case; another later report said there were no confirmed human cases. The Rustler could not locate a current EOHU release resolving that conflict, so this edition is not reporting a human case as confirmed.

The confirmed public-health message is unchanged: the virus is present and residents should continue taking precautions while mosquitoes remain active.
