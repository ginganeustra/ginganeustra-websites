Council puts brakes on another full Notre-Dame shutdown

Staff planned to close the next stretch beginning around Sept. 21. Councillors want the cost, timing and community impact of keeping one lane open.

By The Rustler staff · Published September 5, 2026 · Updated 4:25 p.m.

EMBRUN — Another full shutdown of Notre-Dame Street is no longer a foregone conclusion after Russell Township councillors pushed administration to come back with the costs, timing and consequences of keeping one lane open.

Township staff told council at its Aug. 31 meeting that the next section of the major Notre-Dame reconstruction project — from St-Pierre toward Domaine — was expected to begin around Sept. 21 and continue until late October. The plan presented to council called for another complete road closure while crews replace the watermain and storm sewer.

That immediately drew questions from councillors who have spent the summer hearing from residents and businesses affected by construction.

The original construction contract contemplated keeping one lane of Notre-Dame open. Staff told council circumstances changed after Enbridge imposed requirements to protect a natural-gas main that were not part of the information available when the job was tendered.

Closing the entire road allows the contractor to excavate and rebuild more efficiently and deal with underground infrastructure at the same time. But when councillors asked what the financial difference was between a complete closure and maintaining one lane, administration could not yet provide a figure.

That became the centre of the debate.

Coun. Charles Armstrong said keeping one lane open appeared to be the only reasonable way to balance the work against the needs of residents and businesses. He warned that the next section contains a substantial number of businesses and said they would be hit hard by another complete closure.

Other councillors pressed the same practical question: if a full closure is faster or cheaper, how much faster and how much cheaper?

Administration agreed to return with more information so council can compare the options before the next phase begins.

Coun. Lisa Deacon said council also needs better information about what construction means to the people living and working beside it. She cited one business that had laid off four employees during the work and said future reports should spell out likely impacts on residents and businesses, not only technical construction details.

There were also concerns about traffic spilling into residential streets. If Notre-Dame is completely closed, the Township’s primary detour would use Routes 300 and 400. Staff said it was considering restricting access through a nearby subdivision to prevent motorists from using residential roads as a shortcut.

That created another problem: blocking cut-through traffic could also make travel less convenient for the people who actually live in the subdivision.

Emergency access was also raised. Staff said fire, ambulance and other emergency services are included when road-closure plans are circulated and adjust their routes accordingly.

Council also wants clear signs showing that Town Hall and affected businesses remain accessible.

The current section of Notre-Dame is expected to reopen to traffic around Sept. 18, weather permitting. Some finishing work will remain, and temporary lane restrictions could still be required.

Three days later, if the schedule holds, excavation would begin on the next section. What that traffic arrangement will look like is now the unanswered question.

Staff is expected to return to council with the alternatives, their costs and their effects. Russell council’s next regular meeting is scheduled for Sept. 14 at 6 p.m.
