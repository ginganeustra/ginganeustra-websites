Council puts brakes on another full Notre-Dame shutdown

Staff planned to close the next stretch beginning around Sept. 21. Councillors want the cost, timing and community impact of keeping one lane open.

By The Rustler staff · September 5, 2026 · Updated 7:00 p.m.

EMBRUN — Another full shutdown of Notre-Dame Street is no longer a foregone conclusion after Russell Township councillors pushed administration to come back with the costs, timing and community impact of keeping one lane open.

Staff told council Aug. 31 that excavation on the next stretch, from St-Pierre toward Domaine, is targeted to begin around Sept. 21 and run to late October. The plan presented at the meeting was to close both lanes while crews replace the watermain and storm sewer.

“Keeping one lane open, I think, is the only reasonable way of getting this done,” Coun. Charles Armstrong told council, saying the Township has to balance construction against the needs of residents and businesses.

Infrastructure staff said the fastest construction pattern is a complete closure. “To complete the work, we are planning to fully close the road again for that segment,” council was told.

The complication is that the original contract was bid with one lane remaining open. Staff said requirements to protect an Enbridge gas main were added after tendering, changing how the contractor can work around the underground utilities.

Armstrong was not persuaded that efficiency alone answered the public-interest question. “There are a good number of businesses down here,” he said, adding that they were going to “suffer a fair bit” if access is cut again.

Councillors repeatedly asked what the full closure actually saves — in dollars and in days. Staff did not have that calculation at the meeting.

“I’ll have to come back. I’ll have to ask the team to give me and to review all the figures.” — Township infrastructure staff

That answer effectively changed the direction of the discussion. Council did not simply endorse another complete closure. Instead, staff was asked to return with enough information to compare the options before the next phase starts.

Mayor Mike Tarnowski said council needs the “best possible information” before giving direction, particularly with the Sept. 21 target approaching. The practical question is what one-lane traffic would cost, how much time it would add, and what it would mean for the contractor’s ability to protect the gas main.

Councillors also raised the effect on nearby subdivisions. If the road is completely closed, the main detour is expected to use Routes 300 and 400. Staff said it could block a residential shortcut that drivers used during an earlier closure, but doing that would also make travel less direct for people who live there.

The current section of Notre-Dame is expected to reopen around Sept. 18, weather permitting. Some finishing work will continue, and temporary alternating-lane traffic may still be required.

Three days later, if the schedule holds, crews are supposed to move into the next section. Council’s next regular meeting is Sept. 14, leaving one meeting to settle the traffic plan before the proposed start.
