Embrun water tower delayed again

The tower is now expected back in service near the end of September. Restrictions remain while more extensive work is completed.

By The Rustler staff · September 5, 2026 · Updated 7:21 p.m.

EMBRUN — Residents waiting for normal water service will have to wait longer after another delay in the work on the Embrun water tower.

Administration told council Aug. 31 that the most recent target — the end of August — has slipped by roughly a month while the contractor completes repairs and testing.

“We’re now looking at the end of September,” infrastructure staff told council. The restrictions have to remain until the tower is back online and the system is safe.

The tower has been out of service since October 2025. The Township initially imposed a full outdoor-use ban to preserve enough water for household demand and firefighting, then later relaxed the rules to permit some outdoor use on alternating days.

Staff said the job has not gone according to the original timetable. “The work to be completed did take more time and it’s more extensive than anticipated at first,” council was told.

The delay reopened a question that has been simmering among residents: if service has been restricted for so long, should Embrun water customers receive some money back?

Coun. Charles Armstrong asked staff to examine a one-time rebate, pointing out that much of a household water bill is a fixed charge rather than consumption. “The service wasn’t available and the full service was paid for,” he said.

Administration cautioned that the fixed charge is part of the money that supports the water system whether or not residents are watering lawns. A reduction would lower the transfer to the asset-replacement reserve, creating a shortfall that would have to be made up somewhere else.

“Someone has to pay for it. It’s not money that’s disappeared.” — Township administration

Mayor Mike Tarnowski also noted that the Russell water tower will need work of its own. He suggested council may eventually be better off looking at both projects together so one community is not treated differently from the other.

No rebate was approved Aug. 31. The immediate issue is the September target: until the tower is returned to service, the current restrictions stay in place.
