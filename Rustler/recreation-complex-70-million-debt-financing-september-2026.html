Russell spreads $70-million rec-complex debt to cut interest risk

Township says mixing shorter and longer loans could save about $2.3 million in the first five years while avoiding one giant refinancing date.

By The Rustler staff · Published September 5, 2026 · Updated 4:25 p.m.

EMBRUN — Russell Township will not put all of the recreation complex’s roughly $70 million in long-term borrowing into one big 30-year loan.

Council instead approved a financing strategy Aug. 31 that spreads the debt across different borrowing periods in an effort to take advantage of lower shorter-term interest rates without exposing the entire project to refinancing at the same time.

The plan includes $17.5 million on a five-year term with a 30-year amortization, $24.5 million on a 10-year term with a 30-year amortization, and a longer-term portion of the debt. Council also authorized an initial $17.5-million Infrastructure Ontario pre-flow loan.

Administration told council that, at current Infrastructure Ontario rates, the diversified approach is expected to produce approximately $2.3 million in net interest savings during the first five years compared with putting the borrowing into a single long-term structure.

The Township has already moved to secure a rate of about 3.67 per cent for the initial $17.5-million pre-flow loan.

That raised a governance question because the rate was locked before the full borrowing decision came before council. Mayor Mike Tarnowski said the move was made because rates were rising and waiting until another council meeting could have cost the Township a favourable rate. Council was told the action did not commit Russell to the entire $70-million borrowing package, although backing away from the secured financing could carry a penalty.

Staff said Infrastructure Ontario’s pre-flow process normally takes six to eight weeks and requires a rate to be secured before council approves the final debenture bylaw.

During the discussion, councillors explored what the savings might look like over a longer period. Figures approaching $5 million over 10 years were discussed if future refinancing conditions remain favourable, but those are scenarios rather than guaranteed savings because nobody knows what interest rates will be when the five-year borrowing comes due.

Coun. Charles Armstrong said short-term rates would have to rise substantially before the Township lost the advantage of the strategy. “I think it’s prudent,” he said.

For 2026, debt-servicing costs will come from the recreation-complex reserve. How the Township pays the debt beginning in 2027 will be presented as part of next year’s municipal budget.

The numbers are large even by municipal standards. The Township’s 2026 approved budget had already identified recreation-complex borrowing as one of the municipality’s major financial commitments.
