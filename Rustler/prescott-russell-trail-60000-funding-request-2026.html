Trail group seeks another $60,000 from Prescott-Russell

The counties want more information before deciding on a new funding arrangement for the 72-kilometre recreational trail.

By The Rustler staff · September 5, 2026 · Updated 7:00 p.m.

The organization operating the Prescott and Russell Recreational Trail is asking the United Counties for an additional $60,000 as the two sides prepare to negotiate a new agreement.

The 72-kilometre multi-use route runs from the Ottawa boundary east toward St-Eugène and connects into local trail networks used by walkers, runners, cyclists and snowmobilers.

“Our trail is the spine, the main highway for the snowmobile season,” executive director Eric Collard told county council as he made the funding case.

Management was transferred by the United Counties of Prescott and Russell to an arm’s-length corporation in 2022. Collard asked for public funding equivalent to roughly one dollar for every UCPR resident and said the organization has also been developing corporate sponsorship.

The counties did not simply approve the request. Councillors wanted a clearer picture of what the trail needs, what the new agreement should look like and how the money fits into the 2027 budget.

Collard argued the public investment does more than maintain a recreational path. “That money goes back to the community in multiple ways,” he said.

The proposed Alto high-speed rail corridor is another uncertainty hanging over trails and transportation corridors in Eastern Ontario. The trail corporation is watching that process while trying to secure a more stable local funding arrangement.

For Russell and Embrun residents, the regional funding question has a direct local connection: the trail system is part of everyday recreation and active transportation between the two communities.
