Tarnowski presses UCPR over another delay at new Residence

The new Prescott and Russell Residence has its occupancy permit, but provincial concerns about the nurse-call system are still holding up the move-in.

By The Rustler staff · September 6, 2026 · Updated 10:46 a.m.

The long-delayed new Prescott and Russell Residence has cleared one of its biggest hurdles, but residents still do not have a firm move-in date.

Russell Township Mayor Mike Tarnowski pressed United Counties of Prescott and Russell administration for an update during the Aug. 26 county council meeting after a new “red flag” appeared in the project’s opening schedule.

UCPR Chief Administrative Officer Stéphane Parisien told council the Town of Hawkesbury has now issued an occupancy permit for the new long-term-care home on Spence Street.

That does not mean residents can move in yet.

According to the council discussion reported by Le Reflet/The News, Ontario’s Ministry of Long-Term Care is still not satisfied with the building’s nurse-call system. UCPR was given another three weeks to bring the system into compliance with ministry requirements.

Parisien told council his best estimate was that the new Residence should open to residents by the end of November “at the latest,” and possibly earlier.

The opening has already been delayed repeatedly. Earlier this year, the project was held up by problems with its fire-alarm system. UCPR said in March that additional audible devices had to be installed in resident rooms before an occupancy permit could be obtained.

That work has now been completed and the occupancy permit issued.

UCPR’s social-services department is preparing the logistics for moving residents from the existing Cartier Boulevard home to the new facility once the remaining approvals are in place.

Administration has promised county council further updates as the opening date becomes clearer.
