CANADA LOSES 42,000 JOBS — BEFORE THE NEWEST U.S. TARIFFS ENTERED THE DATA

Full-time employment accounted for most of the decline and unemployment held at 6.4%. The survey reference week ran Aug. 9–15, before Washington’s latest 50% tariffs took effect Aug. 22.

Published Sept. 4, 2026 · 11:50 a.m. ET

Canada lost 42,000 jobs in August, with most of the decline coming from full-time work, while the national unemployment rate held at 6.4 per cent. The result was substantially weaker than economists had expected after several months of employment growth.

Reuters’ survey of economists had pointed to an increase of about 15,000 jobs. Its seasonally adjusted calculation put the actual decline at 41,700, including 35,900 full-time positions and 5,800 part-time positions; Statistics Canada reports the overall change as 42,000 after rounding.

The employment rate — the share of the population aged 15 and older that is working — fell 0.1 percentage points to 60.8 per cent. Youth unemployment was little changed at 12.9 per cent, while average hourly wages were 2.0 per cent higher than a year earlier, down from 2.8 per cent annual growth in July.

The timing of the survey separates these numbers from the newest round of U.S. tariffs. The August Labour Force Survey reference week ran from Aug. 9 through Aug. 15; Washington’s 50-per-cent tariffs on C$27.6 billion of Canadian goods did not take effect until Aug. 22.

Friday’s report therefore does not measure job losses caused by the Aug. 22 tariffs. It provides a labour-market reading immediately before that escalation, giving future surveys a cleaner point of comparison as the new duties and Canada’s Sept. 8 counter-tariffs work through affected industries.

Ontario and Quebec accounted for most of the provincial decline. Employment fell by 18,000 in Ontario and 19,000 in Quebec, while New Brunswick gained about 2,400 jobs and employment was little changed in the other provinces.

The industry numbers moved in different directions. Manufacturing employment increased by 22,000, while business, building and other support services lost 20,000 jobs, public administration lost 8,800, natural resources lost 7,700 and utilities lost 5,600.

Public-sector employment fell by another 20,000 in August, its third consecutive monthly decline, and Statistics Canada says it has fallen by 78,000 since May. Despite the August drop, total employment remained 217,000 higher than a year earlier.

A separate August indicator pointed toward stronger activity. The seasonally adjusted Ivey Purchasing Managers Index rose to 64.3 from 55.1 in July, its highest level since May 2022, while its employment gauge increased to 55.0 from 52.2.

The two measures answer different questions. The Labour Force Survey estimates how many people are employed across the population, while the Ivey index records month-to-month changes reported by purchasing managers; stronger purchasing activity can therefore coexist with a decline in total employment.

Other recent indicators have also been mixed, leaving no single August measure that describes the entire economy. What Friday’s labour report establishes is narrower: employment weakened before the latest U.S. tariff round was present in the survey period, with the largest provincial losses in Ontario and Quebec and manufacturing employment moving in the opposite direction.
