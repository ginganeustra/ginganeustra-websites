Trump confrontation lifts Carney Liberals to 10-point lead: Abacus

Published Aug. 30, 2026 · 7:29 p.m. ET

A new national poll suggests the confrontation with Donald Trump is strengthening Mark Carney’s Liberals politically, with the governing party opening a 10-point lead over the Conservatives as Canadians put the U.S. president back near the top of the national agenda.

The Abacus Data survey, released Sunday, puts the Liberals at 45 per cent among committed voters, the Conservatives at 35 per cent, the NDP at nine per cent and the Bloc Québécois at seven per cent. The Green Party is at two per cent and the People’s Party at one per cent.

The headline numbers: Liberals 45%, Conservatives 35%. Government approval 56%. Sixty-two per cent say the Liberals are the party best able to deal with Trump and his administration.

The Liberal advantage has grown from seven points in Abacus’s previous survey. Liberal support is up three points since Aug. 12, while Conservative support is unchanged at 35 per cent.

The timing is politically important. Abacus surveyed 2,022 Canadian adults from Aug. 21 to Aug. 26, a period that covered the collapse of Canada-U.S. trade negotiations and the implementation of new American tariffs. Abacus says most interviews were completed after those developments.

The movement is not limited to voting intention. Government approval rose four points to 56 per cent, while disapproval fell to 29 per cent. Carney’s personal numbers also improved: 53 per cent of respondents had a positive impression of the prime minister and 28 per cent a negative one, for a net rating of plus 25.

The most revealing result for the Canada-U.S. confrontation may be the change in what voters say they are worried about.

Forty-five per cent of Canadians now identify Trump and his administration as one of the three most important issues facing Canada, up seven points in two weeks. Only the cost of living, at 62 per cent, ranks higher. The economy is third at 39 per cent.

That concern strongly favours the Liberals. Among respondents who identify Trump as a top issue, 60 per cent say they would vote Liberal and 21 per cent Conservative. Asked which party is best able to deal with Trump and his administration, 62 per cent choose the Liberals, compared with 18 per cent who choose the Conservatives.

Those numbers do not prove that every recent Liberal gain was caused by the trade confrontation. Polling captures association, not a laboratory experiment. Cost of living remains the dominant public concern, and federal voting intentions can shift quickly.

But the pattern is difficult to ignore. As Trump has become more salient, the Liberals’ advantage has widened, government approval has risen and Carney’s personal standing has improved.

That presents Pierre Poilievre and the Conservatives with a strategic problem. A government can normally be expected to take political damage when a major trading relationship deteriorates and economic risks rise. So far, Abacus is finding the opposite: voters who are most worried about the U.S. president are disproportionately consolidating behind the governing party.

For Carney, the political benefit carries its own risk. Public support for his handling of Trump creates an expectation that firmness will produce a durable Canadian strategy, not simply better polling. The government still faces the harder work of protecting jobs, diversifying trade and negotiating with Washington without accepting terms it considers damaging to Canadian sovereignty.

Abacus notes that its online panel survey is not a probability sample. For comparison, a random probability sample of the same size would have a margin of error of about plus or minus 2.2 percentage points, 19 times out of 20.

For now, however, the federal political picture is clear: the more Donald Trump dominates Canada’s public agenda, the more favourable the terrain appears to become for Mark Carney’s Liberals.

The next test will be whether that advantage survives a prolonged economic confrontation rather than the initial political rally around it.

Source

  • Abacus Data — national federal tracking survey, Aug. 21–26, 2026

Polling analysis. Percentages are reported from Abacus Data’s published results; causal interpretations are treated cautiously because the survey measures public opinion rather than experimental effects.
