MAGAHEADS: BESSENT CALLS CANADA A ‘LITTLE YIPPY DOG’ AS COUNTER-TARIFFS NEAR

On Fox News, the U.S. Treasury secretary compared Canada to a barking dachshund and said Ottawa’s counter-tariffs would have little effect on American prices.

Published Sept. 6, 2026 · 4:34 p.m. ET

WASHINGTON — U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent used a dog-park analogy to describe Canada in a Fox News interview, comparing the country to a “little yippy dog” as its new counter-tariffs approach.

Speaking with Lara Trump, Bessent described a dachshund that repeatedly antagonized a German shepherd, then said the comparison reminded him of the Canada–U.S. trade fight. He also said Prime Minister Mark Carney had declined an opportunity for a trade deal for political reasons.

Bessent called the expected effect on U.S. prices “negligible” and said he would welcome evidence of a statistically significant price impact. His remarks did not announce a new U.S. tariff measure.

Canada’s Finance Department says its counter-tariffs will take effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on Sept. 8. The measures apply rates of 15, 25 and 50 per cent to U.S.-origin goods selected from products targeted by U.S. Section 338 and Section 232 tariffs, covering $27.6 billion in imports from the United States.

Earlier this week, Carney said Canada remained ready for a trade agreement that benefits both countries. The gap between that position and Bessent’s public characterization now sits alongside the tariff schedule already set by Ottawa.
