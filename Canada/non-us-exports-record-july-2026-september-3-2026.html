NON-U.S. EXPORTS HIT RECORD $25.6 BILLION AS U.S.-BOUND SHIPMENTS FALL 6.6%

Exports outside the United States rose for a third consecutive month and accounted for 33.7% of Canadian merchandise exports in July. The figures show a sharp monthly shift, but commodity movements remain an important part of the change.

Published Sept. 3, 2026 · 5:32 p.m. ET

OTTAWA — Canadian merchandise exports to the United States fell 6.6% in July while exports to countries other than the United States rose 7.4% to a record $25.6 billion, Statistics Canada reported Thursday.

The non-U.S. increase was the third consecutive monthly rise. Statistics Canada said countries other than the United States accounted for 33.7% of Canada's merchandise exports in July. That means roughly two-thirds of merchandise exports still went to the U.S. market, but the monthly non-U.S. share was substantially larger than Canada's long-standing pattern of sending about three-quarters of goods exports south of the border.

Total Canadian merchandise exports fell 2.3% in July and imports rose 2.2%. Canada's merchandise trade surplus with the world narrowed from $4.2 billion in June to $769 million in July, the fifth consecutive monthly surplus.

The U.S. side of the ledger weakened sharply. Exports to the United States recorded their steepest percentage decline since April 2025. Imports from the United States rose 1.8%, and Canada's merchandise trade surplus with the U.S. narrowed from $10.3 billion in June to $5.9 billion in July, the smallest since February.

Statistics Canada attributed much of the decline in U.S.-bound exports to lower shipments of crude oil and gold. Crude-oil exports fell 5.6% in July. Exports of metal and non-metallic mineral products fell 8.5%, with unwrought precious metals among the categories pulling the total down.

The non-U.S. increase was also concentrated in particular products and destinations. Statistics Canada identified higher exports to the Netherlands, including iron ores, nuclear fuel and crude oil; higher shipments to China across several product categories; and increased copper-ore exports to Germany.

Canola was another notable contributor. Canola exports rose 43.2% in July, with higher shipments to China, Pakistan and Japan. For the first seven months of 2026, canola exports were 32.2% higher than in the same period a year earlier, according to Statistics Canada.

The July figures do not establish that the current Canada-U.S. trade war caused the shift. The reference month ended before the Aug. 21 collapse in the latest bilateral negotiations and before the new U.S. tariffs that followed. Commodity prices, shipment timing and destination-specific demand can move monthly trade figures substantially.

Reuters reported that Export Development Canada chief economist Stuart Bergman viewed a U.S. export share below 70% as encouraging for diversification while emphasizing the geographic and commercial pull of the American market. Canada's integrated supply chains, proximity and decades of cross-border investment continue to make the United States Canada's dominant export destination.

The federal government has set a goal of doubling Canada's non-U.S. exports over the next decade as part of a wider effort to reduce economic dependence on a single trading partner. July's data provide a measurable increase in sales to other markets, but they do not show on their own whether the shift will persist or how much of it reflects permanent changes in Canadian trade relationships rather than monthly commodity movements.

The next test will come in the August and September trade data, which will begin to capture shipments made around the latest tariff escalation. Statistics Canada is scheduled to release August merchandise-trade figures on Oct. 6.
