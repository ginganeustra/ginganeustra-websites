Mexico · North America

Mexico thought Canada was about to make a deal. Now the two countries are testing different strategies.

← Canada at War front page

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum has revealed that when she spoke with Mark Carney shortly before the Canada-U.S. negotiations collapsed, the Canadian side appeared headed toward an agreement.

Sheinbaum described the subsequent breakdown not as a rupture but as a desencuentro — a disagreement or falling-out — and said Mexico remains optimistic it can reach its own arrangement with Washington. Economy Minister Marcelo Ebrard is in the United States pursuing that objective.

The contrast is becoming one of the most important subplots in North American trade. Mexico faces 50% U.S. tariffs on steel and aluminum and 25% tariffs on vehicles, yet is emphasizing negotiation and accommodation. Canada has suspended talks and announced matching retaliation after deciding the American package was not in its national interest.

That does not mean Mexico is capitulating or Canada is permanently confrontational. Both governments are trying to protect deeply integrated economies with enormous U.S. exposure. But the two USMCA partners are providing a real-time test of different negotiating strategies toward the same administration.

The outcome matters beyond tariffs. If Washington increasingly deals with Canada and Mexico separately rather than treating USMCA as an integrated three-country framework, the political foundations of North American economic integration could begin changing even before the formal review process settles the agreement’s future.

Source: Official transcript of President Claudia Sheinbaum’s Aug. 24 press conference, Presidency of Mexico.
