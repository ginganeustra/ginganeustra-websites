Washington takes a strategic stake in Vancouver-based Trilogy Metals.

The U.S. Department of War has signed definitive agreements for a US$35.6-million investment that gives Washington ownership and influence in the Canadian-headquartered mining company.

Published Aug. 29, 2026 · Updated Aug. 29, 2026

The U.S. Department of War has signed definitive agreements to invest US$35.6 million in Vancouver-based Trilogy Metals, giving Washington a direct 10 per cent stake in the TSX-listed company and warrants that could lift its economic position to 17.5 per cent.

The agreements go beyond a passive investment. The U.S. government will have the right to designate an independent director to Trilogy’s board until October 2028 and appoint a non-voting board observer while it continues to hold at least eight million shares. Trilogy has also agreed not to incur more than US$1 billion in new third-party debt without Department of War approval through the beginning of 2029.

The money is intended to support exploration, engineering and infrastructure connected to the Upper Kobuk Mineral Projects in northwest Alaska. Those deposits include copper, cobalt, germanium and other minerals Washington considers strategically important to the American defence industrial base and to efforts to reduce dependence on foreign mineral supply chains.

The resources themselves are in the United States, not Canada. That distinction matters: the transaction does not give Washington control over Canadian mineral deposits. But Trilogy is headquartered in Vancouver and publicly traded in Canada, making the structure notable as the United States increasingly uses state capital to secure strategic materials.

The arrangement also shows how industrial policy is moving beyond subsidies, tax credits and procurement. Washington is taking an ownership position, obtaining warrants and securing governance rights in a company that controls access to strategically important projects. That gives the U.S. government a direct financial interest in the development of the assets rather than merely supporting a private operator from the sidelines.

For Canada, the deal is a reminder that the competition over critical minerals is becoming more state-directed. Ottawa has identified critical minerals as central to economic security, defence supply chains and trade diversification. The Trilogy transaction shows how aggressively the United States is prepared to deploy public money to shape those same supply chains.

The definitive agreements were announced Friday and convert a previously proposed investment into a binding transaction. Closing is expected in September, subject to customary conditions.
