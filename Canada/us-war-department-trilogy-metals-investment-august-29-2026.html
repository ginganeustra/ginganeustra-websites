Washington takes a strategic stake in Vancouver-based Trilogy Metals.

The U.S. Department of War has signed definitive agreements for a US$35.6-million investment that gives Washington ownership and influence in the Canadian-headquartered mining company.

Published Aug. 29, 2026 · 12:53 p.m. ET

The U.S. Department of War has signed definitive agreements to invest US$35.6 million in Vancouver-based Trilogy Metals, giving Washington a direct 10 per cent stake in the TSX-listed company and warrants that could lift its economic position to 17.5 per cent.

The agreement also gives the U.S. government the right to designate an independent Trilogy director until October 2028 and appoint a non-voting board observer while it holds at least eight million shares. Trilogy also agreed not to take on more than US$1 billion in third-party debt without Department of War approval through the beginning of 2029.

The money will support exploration, engineering and infrastructure at the Upper Kobuk Mineral Projects in northwest Alaska, which contain copper, cobalt, germanium and other minerals Washington considers strategically important to its defence industrial base. The deposits themselves are American, not Canadian.

But the transaction is notable for Canada because Washington is using state capital to secure critical-mineral supply chains through an ownership position in a Canadian-headquartered public company. The definitive agreements, announced Friday, turn a previously proposed investment into a binding transaction expected to close in September.
