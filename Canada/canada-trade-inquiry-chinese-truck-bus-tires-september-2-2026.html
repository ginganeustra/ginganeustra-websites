CANADA OPENS A TRADE INQUIRY INTO CHINESE TRUCK AND BUS TIRES

A complaint from Canadian retread manufacturers and Michelin Canada has started a formal anti-dumping and subsidy process. It is an inquiry—not yet a finding or a tariff decision.

Published Sept. 2, 2026 · 6:23 p.m. ET

Canada’s trade-remedy system has opened a new case involving truck and bus tires imported from China. The Canadian International Trade Tribunal says it has begun a preliminary injury inquiry after the Canada Border Services Agency initiated investigations into alleged dumping and subsidizing.

The complaint was brought by the Canadian Retread Manufacturers Association of Vancouver and Michelin North America (Canada) Inc. of New Glasgow, Nova Scotia. They allege that the domestic industry has suffered injury because of the imported tires.

The crucial point is procedural. The Tribunal has not found that dumping or subsidizing occurred, and the government notice does not announce a new tariff, a fine or a penalty against a named company. The inquiry is the first injury test in the process set out by the Special Import Measures Act.

By October 30, the Tribunal is to decide whether there is a reasonable indication that the alleged dumping or subsidizing has caused injury or retardation, or threatens to cause injury. If it finds that threshold met, the CBSA will continue its investigations and is scheduled to make preliminary determinations by November 30.

Those later determinations are where possible trade remedies become concrete. If the CBSA makes preliminary dumping or subsidy determinations, the Tribunal will open a final injury inquiry at the same time. Until then, the allegation remains unproven and the case remains preliminary.

That distinction matters because trade cases are often described as though a government has already imposed punishment. Here, Canada has begun examining a complaint about a class of imports from China. The process can lead to anti-dumping or countervailing duties if the statutory tests are met, but this notice is not the final decision.

The Tribunal is an independent quasi-judicial body that reports to Parliament through the Minister of Finance. Its notice says interested persons, associations and governments can apply to participate in the inquiry.
