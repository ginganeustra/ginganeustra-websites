GM puts C$144 million behind Oshawa as U.S. tariff threat hangs over autos.

The next-generation heavy-duty GMC Sierra is slated for Canadian production under a tentative GM–Unifor agreement.

Published Aug. 29, 2026 · 4:50 p.m. ET

General Motors plans to invest C$144 million to add the next-generation heavy-duty GMC Sierra to its Oshawa, Ont., assembly plant, a new Canadian production commitment made while the auto sector remains under intense pressure from U.S. tariffs.

The investment is contained in a tentative collective agreement between GM and Unifor covering more than 4,600 workers in Ontario. The agreement still requires ratification by union members voting this weekend.

According to Unifor’s bargaining report, GM also pledged not to immediately sell or close its CAMI assembly plant in Ingersoll. Roughly 30 per cent of Unifor’s GM membership was on layoff when negotiations opened earlier this month.

The timing is significant. Canadian-built vehicles currently face a 25 per cent U.S. tariff, and Canada-U.S. negotiations ended last week without resolving duties on medium- and heavy-duty vehicles. President Donald Trump has threatened to raise tariffs on Canadian automotive products and steel to 50 per cent beginning Jan. 1, 2027.

For Canada, the Oshawa commitment is a tangible vote for maintaining domestic production despite that uncertainty. But it is not yet a guarantee: the labour agreement must be ratified, and the economics of cross-border vehicle production could change sharply if Washington follows through on its threatened tariff escalation.
