TRUMP SAYS U.S. BANKS ARE BARRED FROM CANADA. FIFTEEN ARE ALREADY HERE.

Trump said Canada does not allow American banks to operate there. AP found 15 U.S.-based banks already doing business in Canada, and Canada’s banking regulator publishes a formal route for foreign branches.

Published Sept. 4, 2026 · 11:50 a.m. ET

WASHINGTON — Donald Trump said this week that Canada does not allow American banks to operate there. The claim is false: U.S.-based banks already operate in Canada as branches and subsidiaries, and Canada’s federal banking regulator publishes a formal process for foreign banks seeking to establish branches.

Trump made the claim Wednesday while answering a question about renewed Canada-U.S. trade negotiations during an Oval Office meeting with travel-industry executives. “We don’t have our banks there. You know why? They don’t allow it,” he said.

An Associated Press fact check found 15 U.S.-based banks operating in Canada as branches or subsidiaries. They include JPMorgan Chase, Citibank, Bank of America, Capital One and Wells Fargo, among other institutions serving Canadian corporate, institutional or consumer markets.

Trump also referred to the Bank of Canada while talking about Canadian banks. The Bank of Canada is the country’s central bank, responsible for monetary policy and financial-system functions; it is not a commercial bank competing for deposits and loans in the United States.

Canada does regulate foreign banks differently depending on how they enter the market. Foreign-owned subsidiaries can operate as Canadian-incorporated Schedule II banks, while authorized foreign bank branches operate under Schedule III of the Bank Act.

The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions maintains a guide specifically for establishing a foreign bank branch in Canada. Full-service foreign branches face restrictions on some deposit-taking activities and lending branches have narrower powers, but those rules are a regulatory framework, not a prohibition on American banks.

The Canadian Bankers Association told AP that U.S.-based bank branches and subsidiaries hold about C$124.6 billion in assets in Canada. Federal Reserve data cited by AP also show eight Canadian banks operating in the United States, so the cross-border banking relationship runs in both directions.

The number of large American retail-bank storefronts in Canada is smaller than the U.S. banking industry’s size might suggest. Banking specialists cited by AP pointed to the size of the Canadian market, established domestic competitors and regulatory costs as factors affecting where foreign institutions choose to build retail networks.

The factual distinction matters if banking becomes part of the trade dispute. Canada can be criticized for particular banking rules or market-access conditions, but that is different from saying American banks are not allowed into the country when multiple U.S. institutions already operate under the existing regime.

Trump did not identify a U.S. bank that Canada had refused to admit, and the White House did not provide AP with evidence supporting his claim. The public regulatory record instead shows an established foreign-bank licensing system and existing American participants.
