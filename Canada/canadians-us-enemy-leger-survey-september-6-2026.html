41% OF CANADIANS NOW CALL THE UNITED STATES AN ENEMY, LÉGER FINDS

A Léger survey finds a sharp rise in the share of Canadians calling the United States an enemy—and support for counter-tariffs even among people expecting higher prices.

Published Sept. 6, 2026 · 4:35 p.m. ET

OTTAWA — Forty-one per cent of Canadians now describe the United States as an enemy country, according to a Léger survey released Friday. That is 15 percentage points higher than in June 2025.

In the survey, 22 per cent called the United States an ally and another 22 per cent called it neutral. Asked whether their impression of the United States would change if Donald Trump were no longer president, 64 per cent said it would, while 21 per cent said it would not.

The poll also found broad resistance to Canada becoming a U.S. state: 85 per cent said they would not want that outcome, while 9 per cent said they would.

Trade costs did not erase support for Ottawa’s response. Léger reported that 74 per cent agreed with the federal government’s decision to impose new tariffs on selected U.S. products. Among respondents asked the tariff questions, 78 per cent expected higher consumer prices, but 66 per cent said they would still support the measures even if some prices rose.

Sixty-three per cent expected the new tariffs to have a major or moderate effect on their own finances, and 87 per cent expected at least some effect. Canada’s Finance Department says the measures are scheduled to begin at 12:01 a.m. ET on Sept. 8.

Léger conducted the online survey Aug. 28–31 among 1,552 Canadian adults recruited from its LEO panel and weighted the results by demographic factors. The firm says a probability sample of that size would have a comparison margin of error of no more than 2.48 percentage points, 19 times out of 20.
