Trade talks · Briefing

Ottawa sees the first U.S. retreat on the French-language fight.

← Canada at War front page

Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc says Canada welcomes what he describes as Washington withdrawing its positions on online discoverability and labelling and confirming that Canadian measures promoting French and Canadian culture will not be targeted by future U.S. trade action.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer frames the episode differently. He says Washington had concerns and discussed them, but showed substantial flexibility and never regarded the issue as remotely important enough to sacrifice a good agreement.

The disagreement over what happened remains. The more important forward-looking sentence came from LeBlanc: Ottawa is inviting further U.S. clarifications on other positions that could create the possibility of a mutually beneficial agreement respecting Canadian sovereignty.

That is not a restart of negotiations. Greer says there is currently no open communication channel. But it is the clearest public indication yet of what a path back might look like: Washington retreats or clarifies disputed positions, and Ottawa decides whether enough has changed to return.

Sources: Statement by Dominic LeBlanc, Aug. 27; Reuters interview reporting on U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer.
