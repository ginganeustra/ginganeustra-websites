CFIB REPORTS FINANCIAL PRESSURE AFTER TARIFF ESCALATION

Among businesses affected by the trade war, CFIB says 18% of small exporters and 11% of importers surveyed would no longer be financially viable if it lasted at least three months. The figures are reported expectations, not observed closures.

Published Sept. 3, 2026 · 1:00 p.m. ET

New preliminary results from the Canadian Federation of Independent Business point to mounting concern among small firms after the latest Canada–U.S. tariff escalation. Among surveyed businesses affected by the trade war, CFIB said 18% of small exporters and 11% of importers expected they would cease being financially viable if the situation lasted three months or more.

The survey had 1,545 respondents and was conducted from Aug. 28 onward, after the latest escalation. That timing makes it a newer read on sentiment than the Export Development Canada survey released earlier Wednesday, but the CFIB results measure businesses’ stated assessments of future viability. They are not a count of closures, a representative forecast, or evidence that those firms have already failed.

CFIB said 46% of small exporters and 49% of small importers responding to the survey had products directly affected by the latest tariffs or counter-tariffs. It identified manufacturing, wholesale, retail and construction as among the most affected sectors.

The organization is proposing a dedicated Small Business Tariff Relief program. Under its proposal, eligible exporters and importers that could document direct tariff payments, or price changes made to absorb the tariff, could receive initial relief of up to $70,000.

That is CFIB’s policy proposal, not an approved government benefit. The federation also called for a tariff-remissions desk for small and medium-sized firms and broad tax changes, including a reduction in the small-business corporate tax rate.

CFIB’s release and methodology should be read with the figures: the survey captures the views of firms that responded during an active trade dispute, not the experience of every Canadian small business. Its significance is that the responses were collected after the escalation and quantify the financial strain respondents say they expect if it persists.
