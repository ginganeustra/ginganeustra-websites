New lead · Financial Times

Beware the Angry Canadian.

The Financial Times sees something larger than a tariff fight. Trump’s pressure campaign is producing an unusually broad Canadian coalition — Ford and Joly, Kenney and Rae, Atwood and Neil Young, even Conrad Black — around sovereignty, resilience and the refusal to be pushed around.

The economic imbalance remains formidable. But coercion becomes harder when the population being pressured begins to interpret economic pain as the price of maintaining national independence.

Story 2 · Sovereignty Watch

When a trade war becomes a fight over who gets to determine Canada’s future.

The Guardian’s latest international briefing frames the confrontation as a defence of Canadian sovereignty. Ottawa itself says independence and sovereignty were explicit negotiating objectives. Once cultural policy, language protections and freedom of economic action become part of the argument, a tariff bargain is no longer only about tariff rates.

The political consequence is crucial: economic pressure works differently when voters believe they are paying to defend national autonomy rather than simply absorbing the cost of a commercial disagreement.

Story 3 · Europe

Carney is being handed Europe’s parliamentary microphone.

Carney will attend Ursula von der Leyen’s State of the Union address Sept. 16 and address the European Parliament Sept. 17. European reporting says he will be the first non-European head of state or government to attend the annual State of the Union address in person.

The appearance comes ahead of the Oct. 29-30 EU-Canada summit and after Carney called for a much stronger and deeper economic and security partnership with Europe.

Canada remains profoundly dependent on the U.S. economy. National unity can strengthen negotiating endurance, but it cannot eliminate the costs of a prolonged confrontation.
