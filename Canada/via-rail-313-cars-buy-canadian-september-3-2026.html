OTTAWA COMMITS $4.7 BILLION TO BUILD 313 VIA RAIL CARS IN CANADA

The passenger cars will be produced and assembled in Thunder Bay and Quebec, returning VIA passenger-car production to Canada after four decades as Ottawa expands its Buy Canadian procurement policy.

Published Sept. 3, 2026 · 5:32 p.m. ET

OTTAWA — The federal government will spend more than $4.7 billion for VIA Rail to acquire and maintain 313 new passenger rail cars from Alstom Canada, Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Thursday in Thunder Bay. Ottawa says it is the largest investment in VIA Rail's history and will bring passenger-car production back to Canada for the first time in four decades.

The cars will be manufactured and assembled at Alstom facilities in Thunder Bay, Ontario, and La Pocatière, Quebec, with design and engineering work in Saint-Bruno-de-Montarville, Quebec. The Prime Minister's Office estimates the project will support nearly 700 jobs in Ontario and Quebec and generate more than $1.6 billion in economic benefits.

The 313-car order is intended for VIA's long-distance, regional and remote routes and will include coaches, dining cars, panorama cars and sleepers. Some of the equipment it is expected to replace is more than 70 years old. VIA says much of its stainless-steel long-distance fleet was built by the Budd Company in Philadelphia in 1954 and 1955 for Canadian Pacific and was acquired by VIA when it took over CP passenger operations in 1978.

Carney used the announcement to draw a direct comparison with that history. Reuters quoted him saying that cars previously built in the United States would now be built in Thunder Bay. The Prime Minister's Office also framed the procurement as part of the federal Buy Canadian policy and its effort to reduce dependence on any single trading partner.

Canadian production of VIA passenger cars did not end with the Budd fleet. VIA's LRC coaches were built by Bombardier between 1981 and 1984. Alstom acquired Bombardier Transportation in 2021. The new order therefore marks the return of VIA passenger-car production to Canada after roughly four decades, but it should not be described as Alstom having built VIA's early-1980s fleet under its current corporate ownership.

The government says Alstom is expected to draw on a network of more than 900 Canadian suppliers and to maximize Canadian steel in structural assemblies, supports and fabricated metal components. Those figures and sourcing commitments are government and company projections for the program rather than completed procurement results.

The passenger-car order follows a separate $1.95-billion package announced in July for VIA's locomotive renewal. That package includes $1.6 billion for 45 locomotives from Swiss manufacturer Stadler, with final assembly of up to 36 locomotives in Canada, and $357 million for an assembly and maintenance facility in Montreal. Ottawa says the combined fleet-renewal investment now exceeds $6.6 billion.

The VIA program predates the latest breakdown in Canada-U.S. trade talks. The locomotive program was announced before the Aug. 21 collapse in negotiations, and government materials reviewed for this story do not indicate that VIA cancelled an existing U.S. contract for the 313 passenger cars. Thursday's announcement instead places an existing fleet-renewal need inside the government's broader Buy Canadian industrial strategy.

That announcement came less than two weeks after the United States imposed a new round of tariffs on Canadian goods and Canada announced matching counter-tariffs. Ottawa's current counter-tariff package covers C$27.6 billion of U.S. goods and is scheduled to take effect Sept. 8.

The government has presented federal procurement as one tool for shifting more production, supply contracts and industrial capacity into Canada. The VIA order establishes the spending commitment and the intended Canadian production sites; the jobs, supplier purchases and economic benefits will depend on the program's execution over the life of the contract.
