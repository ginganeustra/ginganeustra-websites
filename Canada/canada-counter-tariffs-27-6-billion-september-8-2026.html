🇨🇦CANADA AT WAR
Trade war · Ottawa responds

Canada answers with $27.6 billion in counter-tariffs.

Ottawa has moved from warning to implementation: matching U.S. tariff rates on a broad list of American goods beginning September 8, while launching a new support package for Canadian workers and businesses.

Published August 25, 2026 · 12:05 ET

Finance Canada says the new countermeasures will cover $27.6 billion in imports from the United States. The tariffs will be set at 15%, 25% and 50%, with each product matched to the corresponding U.S. rate. They take effect at 12:01 a.m. on September 8.

50%

Steel, aluminum, furniture and apparel

Goods moving to the top rate include steel and aluminum products that were previously subject to 25% Canadian counter-tariffs, along with furniture and clothing/apparel.

25%

Appliances, dairy, seafood and derivatives

The 25% list includes appliances, dairy products such as cheese, fish and seafood, and certain steel and aluminum derivative products.

15%

A wider band of targeted U.S. goods

Other products will face a 15% surtax. The detailed Finance Canada schedule runs across sectors including agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, electronics and other industrial and consumer goods.

Autos stay under the existing Canadian regime

Finance Canada says existing Canadian counter-tariffs on U.S. autos remain in place. The new list is therefore additive to measures already imposed earlier in the trade conflict rather than a replacement for the auto response.

$7.5 billion in new support

Ottawa also announced a $7.5 billion package for workers and businesses. The measures include expanded regional tariff-response funding, new BDC liquidity, a Canada Strong Diversification Fund, and employment and training supports intended to help companies and workers absorb the shock and shift toward new markets.

The political message

The government is presenting the response as deliberately reciprocal: “dollar for dollar, rate for rate.” Finance Canada says the U.S. asked too much and offered too little in the failed negotiations, and Ottawa is now trying to protect domestic producers while maintaining room for tariff remission where exceptional relief is warranted.

Foreign reaction: still more commentary than government action

As of midday August 25, there is still no major new formal response from the governments of the European Union, United Kingdom, Mexico or China specifically to Canada’s newly announced counter-tariffs. International media and markets, however, are treating the move as a clear escalation. Reuters describes the package as a significant deterioration in bilateral trade relations, while Asia-Pacific market commentary has linked the Canada-U.S. rupture to broader risk aversion around global trade.

Sources: Department of Finance Canada, “Canada announces targeted countermeasures and substantive support for workers and businesses in response to U.S. tariffs,” Aug. 25, 2026; Department of Finance Canada product list effective Sept. 8; Reuters, Aug. 25, 2026.
