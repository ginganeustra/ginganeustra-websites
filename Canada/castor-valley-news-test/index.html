Russell Township • Council tonight

Russell council meets at 6 p.m. as the 2027 budget consultation enters its final week

Residents have two immediate ways into local decision-making: watch Monday night’s council meeting and submit priorities for next year’s municipal budget before the township’s Sept. 4 survey deadline.
Published Aug. 31, 2026 • Local government desk

Russell Township council is scheduled to meet at 6 p.m. Monday at Town Hall, 717 Notre-Dame St. in Embrun. The township says the meeting will be broadcast on its YouTube channel and has posted an agenda package online. That makes the meeting itself a recurring reporting source: agenda items can be previewed before council sits, decisions can be checked against the livestream, and minutes can be used afterward to confirm the public record.

The meeting arrives as the township asks residents what they want emphasized in the 2027 budget. Its public budget survey remains open until Friday, Sept. 4. The township says responses will be shared with council and can address roads, parks, recreation, emergency services, infrastructure and other spending priorities. A formal public consultation is also scheduled for Dec. 14.

For a local newsroom, this is the kind of routine municipal material that can produce several stories from one cycle: a pre-meeting agenda story, individual reports on major decisions, a budget-priorities explainer and follow-ups when projects move from proposal to tender or construction.

Sources: Township of Russell — Aug. 31 council meeting; Township of Russell — 2027 budget consultation.

Limoges • Infrastructure

$4.8-million Limoges water project includes up to $3.3 million from Ontario

The Nation Municipality says the project will replace water-treatment equipment in Limoges with modern filtration trains, automated controls and stainless-steel construction. The municipality put the project cost at about $4.8 million and provincial support at up to $3.3 million.

Although announced earlier this year, the project remains a useful example of a continuing beat: procurement, construction milestones, disruptions, cost changes and completion can all generate follow-up reporting.

Public-meeting notices flag zoning and variance applications

Russell Township has posted public-meeting notices covering proposed zoning changes at 4513 Grégoire Rd. in Russell and lands in Vars, along with a minor-variance application at 1600 St-Jacques Rd. in Embrun.

These notices are exactly the sort of small-document source a daily local site can turn into readable neighbourhood stories: what is proposed, what rule must change, when residents can comment and what council or the adjustment committee eventually decides.

Growth

Russell Township’s Official Plan covers Embrun, Russell, parts of Limoges and Marionville, plus the Highway 417 Industrial Park. It guides where housing, businesses, roads, water, sewers, parks and other services are expected to grow.

That creates a permanent development beat: every significant rezoning or plan amendment can be tested against the township’s long-term land-use framework.

How the daily source desk would work

The newsroom would treat official pages as primary reporting feeds and local media as attributed leads, not copy to be republished. A morning and afternoon sweep could monitor municipal agendas, minutes, planning notices, tenders, road closures, police and fire releases, school boards, health services, conservation authorities, provincial and federal announcements, MPs/MPPs, candidate pages and community organizations. Council and committee video transcripts add a layer of original reporting that does not depend on anyone issuing a press release.

Facebook groups would be useful mainly as a tip line: complaints, questions, photos, missing context and community chatter would be checked against documents or direct sources before becoming news.
