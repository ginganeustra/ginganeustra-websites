Petitions · Tax policy · Canada–U.S. trade

A petition asks Ottawa to revive the digital-services tax.

Published Aug. 29, 2026 · 8:30 p.m. ET

A newly opened House of Commons petition calls on the Carney government to restore the Digital Services Tax Act, repealed in July 2025 while Canada pursued trade negotiations with the United States. Petition e-7664 argues that the concession produced no durable benefit: U.S. tariffs continued, it says, while Ottawa gave up revenue from large technology companies operating in Canada. The petition asks Parliament to reinstate the tax in the form adopted in 2024 and publish a transparent account of the revenue forgone since repeal. It does not itself change tax law or compel the government to act. But it puts a pointed question on the public record after the collapse of talks: should a tax concession made for a prospective deal remain in place when the promised diplomatic return has not materialized? Any revival would also require Ottawa to weigh renewed U.S. retaliation against fiscal sovereignty, business costs and the policy’s stated aim of taxing digital revenues generated in Canada.
