The Walrus · Aug. 31, 2026

Canada’s Trade War Is a Fight for the Free World

Michael Ondaatje

The Walrus frames Canada’s tariff confrontation with the United States as more than a dispute over duties and market access: it is a test of whether a smaller democracy can resist economic coercion from a much larger ally. The piece argues that Mark Carney’s decision to stand up to Donald Trump offers other countries a model for defending sovereignty when Washington uses trade leverage to force political concessions. Its larger claim is that Canada’s fight matters beyond Canada because the future of the free world depends on countries being able to say no to power without surrendering the rules that are supposed to bind allies.

The Guardian · Aug. 31, 2026

As US-Canada tensions flare, who’s calling the shots in Trump’s trade team?

The Guardian

The Guardian maps the competing power centres around Donald Trump’s Canada policy, focusing on U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and trade adviser Peter Navarro. Its reporting helps explain why Canadian negotiators could believe they had made progress only to encounter harder terms as the deal approached the finish line, because the American officials shaping tariffs did not always agree on what should be conceded. The larger lesson is that Ottawa may be negotiating not with one coherent U.S. position but with a White House system in which authority is divided until Trump makes the final call.

The Wall Street Journal · Aug. 31, 2026

Canadian Small Businesses to Bear Brunt of New U.S. Tariffs

The Wall Street Journal

The Journal shows how a tariff package that affects only a minority of Canada’s total exports can still be devastating when the exposure is concentrated among smaller companies with little room to absorb a 50 per cent duty. Exporters of goods such as honey, wool, cosmetics, flowers and artwork describe a basic arithmetic problem: American customers will not pay the full premium, while the Canadian seller cannot simply erase half its margin. The piece is valuable because it moves the trade war away from national totals and large industrial sectors and into the cash flow, hiring and survival decisions of individual Canadian businesses.
