Canada at War · Foreign reaction

China responds to Canada’s retaliation — carefully.

Beijing’s first direct official response is deliberately neutral: trade disputes, it says, should be settled through equal dialogue and consultation.

← Canada at War front page

China has now responded officially to Canada’s new counter-tariffs against the United States, offering a restrained statement that is significant less for what Beijing endorsed than for what it declined to do.

At the Chinese Foreign Ministry’s regular Aug. 26 briefing, spokesperson Lin Jian was asked directly about Canada’s retaliation. The exchange, translated from the ministry’s official Chinese transcript, was:

Bloomberg: “Canada has retaliated against the tariffs set by President Trump, including imposing 50% tariffs on products such as dairy and steel. What views or suggestions does the Foreign Ministry have regarding measures Canada or other countries might take in response to Trump’s tariff war?”

Lin Jian: “China has consistently maintained that all parties should resolve their respective economic and trade concerns through dialogue and consultation on an equal footing.”

The significance is in the diplomatic positioning. China did not explicitly endorse Canada’s counter-tariffs, did not attack Washington by name in its answer and did not present Canada as an ally. Beijing instead placed the confrontation inside its longstanding argument that economic disputes should be negotiated on an equal basis.

The phrase “equal footing” is nevertheless notable in the present Canadian context. Ottawa says it suspended negotiations rather than accept terms it considered inconsistent with Canada’s interests and sovereignty. Beijing’s formulation implicitly emphasizes negotiation between sovereign equals rather than economic coercion by the larger party, while allowing China to avoid inserting itself directly into a Canada-U.S. dispute.

That makes this an official foreign-government reaction, distinct from the much more expansive commentary appearing in Chinese state-linked media. It should not be overstated as Chinese support for Carney. It is better understood as Beijing taking advantage of the moment to reiterate a principle that aligns with its own criticism of American tariff policy.

Source: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People’s Republic of China, regular press conference, Aug. 26, 2026. The English wording above is a direct translation of the ministry’s published Chinese transcript.
