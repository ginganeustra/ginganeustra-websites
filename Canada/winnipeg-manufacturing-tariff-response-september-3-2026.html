OTTAWA PUTS $15.9 MILLION INTO WINNIPEG MANUFACTURING AFTER U.S. TARIFF ESCALATION

Fifteen projects at 11 Winnipeg organizations will finance machinery, automation, in-house production and expansion into new markets. Some of the federal support is non-repayable; some must be repaid.

Published Sept. 3, 2026 · 2:32 p.m. ET

Ottawa is putting $15.9 million into 15 manufacturing projects led by 11 Winnipeg organizations as part of its response to the latest escalation in the Canada–U.S. tariff dispute.

The money, announced Thursday by Prairies Economic Development Canada, is split between the Regional Tariff Response Initiative and the federal Business Scale-up and Productivity program. The project descriptions cover new machinery, factory automation, digital systems, additional production space, workforce training and efforts to sell into new markets. The financing is not all the same: several Regional Tariff Response Initiative contributions are non-repayable, while other contributions under that program and the productivity program are repayable.

Fort Garry Fire Trucks is receiving two separate contributions. A $999,575 non-repayable tariff-response contribution is intended to bring more component production in-house, including pump components and plastic tanks, while improving digital systems and testing. A second contribution of $1,114,651, which is repayable, is for fabrication, welding, rubber-manufacturing and painting equipment.

Company president James Peters said the financing would help “bring the manufacture of key fire truck components … back into Canada.” The federal backgrounder says those components are currently sourced internationally; it does not say that all of that sourcing is from the United States.

Smartrend Manufacturing Group is also receiving two contributions: $1 million in non-repayable tariff-response funding to expand into new markets, add sales, marketing and regulatory staff and train workers, and $2,087,916 in repayable productivity financing to automate part of its electronics production line and launch two products using its illuminated-sign technology. City Sheet Metal is receiving $570,025 in non-repayable funding to upgrade aluminum and steel production equipment, increase production, train workers and expand sales in Western Canada.

Other projects show the range of industrial activity covered by the announcement. Argus Industries is receiving $507,179 in non-repayable funding to build and equip a 5,000-square-foot production space for specialized aerospace and defence seals. International Truck Body is receiving $1 million in non-repayable support and $1,119,407 in repayable financing for a new step-van manufacturing line. Micro Tool & Machine is receiving $536,105 in non-repayable support to modernize digital planning and production systems.

The federal government says the 15 projects support more than 358 jobs. That figure is the government’s description of jobs associated with the projects; it is not a count of 358 newly created jobs or evidence that every announced expansion has already been completed.

The announcement comes from a province whose manufacturing economy has been tightly linked to the United States for more than three decades. PrairiesCan noted that when the North American Free Trade Agreement took effect in 1994, Manitoba’s exports to the United States grew faster than those of any other province, with machinery and transportation equipment among the areas posting large gains. By December 2025, Manitoba manufacturing employed 67,700 people, about nine per cent of the provincial workforce, according to the federal release.

The federal government placed Thursday’s financing directly inside its account of the current trade conflict. Ottawa says the United States imposed additional tariffs on Canadian goods two weeks ago and that Canada suspended negotiations rather than accept terms the government considered damaging to Canadian workers, strategic industries and sovereignty. Canada has announced matching counter-tariffs. Those descriptions of the negotiating dispute are the federal government’s position; no negotiating text accompanying Thursday’s Winnipeg announcement was released.

The Winnipeg financing is part of a wider support package announced Aug. 25. Ottawa added $1.5 billion to the Regional Tariff Response Initiative and said the measure formed part of $7.5 billion in new and expanded support for tariff-affected workers and businesses. The package also included a $2-billion Canada Strong Diversification Fund, $500 million in new Business Development Bank of Canada liquidity, $3.5 billion in Rapid Response Supports for Workers and Employers and changes to the Large Enterprise Tariff Loan facility.

The project-by-project breakdown shows how some of that tariff-response policy is being translated into factory spending. In Winnipeg, the funded changes include replacing internationally sourced components with Canadian production, adding automated equipment, expanding manufacturing lines and paying companies to pursue customers in different markets. The announcement establishes the financing and the intended projects; the resulting production shifts, sales and employment will depend on their implementation.
