$2.7 billion flows into B.C.'s gas backbone as Canada builds export options.

KKR and Apollo will finance two expansions of Enbridge's Westcoast system. Enbridge keeps majority ownership and operational control while capacity grows for B.C., the U.S. Pacific Northwest and LNG exports.

Published Aug. 28, 2026

Private capital is making a C$2.7-billion bet on the infrastructure carrying Western Canadian natural gas toward domestic customers, the United States and increasingly the Pacific export market.

Enbridge announced a joint venture led by KKR, with Apollo funds participating, to finance the already sanctioned Aspen Point and Sunrise expansion programs on the Westcoast natural gas pipeline system in British Columbia.

The investors will ultimately receive an indirect cumulative 29-per-cent interest in the aggregate Westcoast system after Sunrise enters service. Enbridge retains majority ownership, operational control and responsibility for construction.

Aspen Point is expected to enter service in 2026 and Sunrise in late 2028. Both projects have regulatory approval and are backed by long-term take-or-pay contracts.

A Pacific-facing energy system

The Westcoast network stretches more than 2,900 kilometres from northeastern British Columbia south to the Canada-U.S. border. It currently can move as much as 3.6 billion cubic feet of gas per day; Enbridge says capacity should reach about 3.9 bcf/d after Sunrise enters service.

The system remains deeply integrated with the United States, serving the Pacific Northwest. But its role is changing as B.C. LNG projects create another outlet for Western Canadian gas. Apollo explicitly cited international LNG demand when explaining the investment.

For Canada's diversification strategy, that distinction matters. Reducing dependence on one customer does not require severing continental infrastructure. It requires adding credible alternative markets and the transportation capacity to reach them.

The transaction also demonstrates that Canada's trade-diversification buildout is attracting enormous pools of private capital. KKR and Apollo are not making a symbolic geopolitical gesture; they are investing against contracted infrastructure cash flows. That is precisely why the deal is significant.
