🇨🇦 CANADA AT WAR
Ottawa · Trade war

Canada will unveil its next U.S. tariff response Tuesday.

Finance Canada has scheduled a technical briefing and joint ministerial press conference as Ottawa prepares September 8 counter-tariffs and a major support package for Canadian workers and businesses.

Published Aug. 25, 2026 · 00:14 ET

Ottawa is moving from warning to implementation. Finance Canada says François-Philippe Champagne, Mélanie Joly, Patty Hajdu and Evan Solomon will jointly announce new measures on Tuesday, August 25, aimed at protecting Canadian workers and businesses from the widening U.S. tariff confrontation.

Three moments to watch Tuesday

The government has scheduled a 9:00 a.m. ET technical briefing, followed by a 10:30 a.m. site visit and an 11:00 a.m. press conference. The technical briefing is expected to give reporters the first detailed look at the measures before ministers present them publicly.

A separate Finance Canada readout says Champagne told provincial and territorial finance ministers that the federal government still intends to impose dollar-for-dollar counter-tariffs on September 8. Ottawa is also preparing what it calls a major support package to help firms maintain liquidity, protect workers and strengthen the economy's resilience.

Provinces are being asked what should be hit

Champagne invited provincial and territorial ministers to provide input on proposed countermeasures, the products to be targeted and the supports needed by affected businesses and workers. The discussion also covered Buy Canadian measures, internal trade barriers and export diversification.

The key unresolved question is whether Tuesday's announcement will include the final detailed tariff schedule or primarily the support package. Finance Canada's advisory confirms new measures are coming, but does not itself publish the product list.

That makes Tuesday morning the next major decision point in the confrontation: not another round of rhetoric, but the first detailed look at how Ottawa intends to absorb, redirect and retaliate against the latest U.S. pressure.

Sources: Department of Finance Canada media advisory, Aug. 24, 2026; Finance Canada federal-provincial readout, Aug. 24–25, 2026.
