Grêmio · finance · registration rules

ANRESF restricts Grêmio player registrations, UOL reports.

The club must seek prior authorization before registering players after requesting a collective debt plan. The measure is conditional, not an automatic total transfer ban.

BraGinga news desk · Published 25 August 2026, 15:00 ET / 16:00 BRT

Brazil’s National Agency for Football Regulation and Sustainability has placed a preventive restriction on Grêmio’s ability to register players, according to reporting Tuesday by UOL columnist Rodrigo Mattos.

Under the reported measure, Grêmio may continue negotiating with players, but it must obtain ANRESF authorization before a new registration. UOL reported that the restriction followed the club’s July 31 request for a collective debt-payment plan through the CBF’s National Dispute Resolution Chamber.

Important distinction: this is not an automatic, blanket transfer ban. It is a prior-authorization requirement governing new player registrations.

What approval would require

UOL reported that ANRESF may authorize a registration when Grêmio’s aggregate transfer spending does not exceed player-sale revenue and the move does not increase the club’s player wage bill.

The report also says Grêmio could negotiate a restructuring agreement covering two seasons, with financial conditions replacing the current preventive restriction.

What the rules say

ANRESF’s published solvency guidance says the regulator monitors overdue obligations and may block a club in the national registration system. Its institutional material also says the agency can approve recovery plans and adjustment agreements for clubs in financial difficulty.

ANRESF’s public communications and recent-decision pages did not identify a Grêmio-specific order when BraGinga checked Tuesday afternoon. The club-specific details in this report therefore remain attributed to UOL; neither Grêmio nor ANRESF had published a public response located by this edition’s deadline.

Sources: UOL / Rodrigo Mattos — report on the Grêmio restriction; ANRESF — official solvency rules and registration sanctions; ANRESF — official powers and recovery-plan oversight.
