Le Grand Continent — “Mark Carney’s full speech against the vassalisation of Canada”

The Prime Minister of Canada, Mark Carney, delivered this speech on Saturday, August 22, 2026, at 11 a.m., during a press conference in Ottawa, convened a few hours after Canadian negotiators returned from Washington during the night and a few hours after the American tariffs of 50% came into effect.

The United States had declared that the negotiations had failed because of last-minute Canadian demands. In this speech, Carney retraces the history of the discussions and dismantles the American argument.

The American demands are not consistent with the relationship between two independent states, but instead pursued one of Donald Trump’s stated objectives: turning Canada into the 51st state. Among these demands were the American right to supervise agreements that Canada signs with third countries, the complete dismantling of the supply-management system for dairy products enshrined in Canadian law, changes to language regulations in Quebec, the opening of public procurement markets, the automatic alignment of Canadian customs tariffs with Washington’s trade policy, and even American control over the exploitation of critical minerals and Canada’s defense-spending objectives.

The cumulative impact of this list, as revealed by the Canadian prime minister, is striking. When examined side by side, these demands resemble less the contours of a free-trade agreement than those of a guardianship regime touching practically every domain of Canadian sovereignty: the economy, culture, language, resources, and defense.

It is precisely on this point that Carney chose to respond.

Instead of examining these demands one by one, he redefines them as an attack on sovereignty and transforms the announcement of the breakdown in trade relations into a call for unity of unprecedented scope for a Canadian prime minister.

First, through the vocabulary Carney uses, echoing the expression “masters in our own house” from Quebec’s Quiet Revolution (“In Canada, we are masters in our own house, from coast to coast”), and elevating the defense of the French language to the level of a vital interest of the Federation.

Next, through his target audience, since he addresses the provinces, businesses, consumers—invited to “choose Canada”—and even Americans, described as collateral victims of a war “they did not want,” as well as Europeans, with whom the prime minister calls for the construction of a new agreement.

Finally, by presenting without hesitation his diagnosis that the American tariffs aim to “sow discord” and that unity therefore constitutes the nation’s first line of defense, Mark Carney places himself at the forefront of a Western front of resistance to the Trumpist attempt at vassalization.

In his first reaction to this speech, Donald Trump responded by declaring that Canada wanted “to enjoy the advantages of being a state without being one.”
