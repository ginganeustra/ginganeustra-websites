Selección · End of an era

Messi closes the door: his Argentina career is over.

Monday, 31 August 2026

Published 31 August 2026, 12:35 ET / 13:35 ART

Lionel Messi has retired from international football, ending a national-team career that lasted more than two decades and carried Argentina from years of frustration to the 2022 World Cup title.

Messi announced the decision Monday in a handwritten message published on social media, bringing an end to the most important international career in modern Argentine fútbol. He said he had thought about the decision since the World Cup final and concluded that the moment had arrived.

“Me vacié, ya no tengo más para dar” — “I emptied myself; I have no more to give” — was the line that explained the decision most clearly. Messi also wrote that younger players are coming behind him and deserve their opportunity.

The retirement is from Argentina, not from club football. Messi remains an Inter Miami player. The distinction matters: the Albiceleste has lost its captain and organizing figure, but Messi has not announced that he is finished as a professional.

The ending came after one last World Cup run

Argentina reached the 2026 World Cup final before losing 1–0 to Spain after extra time on July 19. It was Messi’s sixth World Cup and the last international match of his career.

That defeat did not undo the transformation of his relationship with the national team. For much of his career, Messi was measured against Diego Maradona and against his own extraordinary club standards. Argentina lost the 2014 World Cup final and several Copa América finals while Messi carried the burden of being asked why Barcelona’s greatest player could not reproduce the same ending in sky blue and white.

The answer eventually came in trophies. Argentina won the 2021 Copa América, the 2022 Finalissima and, above all, the 2022 World Cup in Qatar. Another Copa América followed in 2024. By the time Argentina returned to the World Cup final in 2026, the old argument about whether Messi had delivered for his country had long since disappeared.

Messi leaves with 207 senior appearances and 125 goals for Argentina, both national records. The numbers describe extraordinary longevity: he remained the centre of the selección from adolescence into his late thirties.

A decision made before the announcement

Messi’s letter added a personal detail that gives the announcement a different weight. He said the words had originally been written on July 21, two days after the World Cup final. The death of his father, Jorge Messi, on Aug. 8 reinforced a decision that had already been forming.

That chronology matters because this was not an impulsive reaction to losing the final. Messi had already begun to close the international chapter while the World Cup was still fresh. Monday’s announcement made public something he had been carrying privately for weeks.

Argentina now has to become something else

The immediate question for the selección is not how to replace Messi, because there is no direct replacement. It is how to reorganize the team without a player who has spent years determining where Argentina’s attacks begin, where opponents concentrate their defenders and who receives the ball when a match slows down.

That transition had already started during the 2026 World Cup, when Argentina used Messi more selectively and developed a broader group around him. But there is a large difference between managing his minutes and knowing he is no longer available at all.

The next Argentina side will belong more clearly to the generation that grew up around him rather than behind him. Julián Álvarez, Lautaro Martínez, Enzo Fernández, Alexis Mac Allister and the players who follow them will inherit a national team whose standards were reset during Messi’s final years.

Messi once walked away from the selección in frustration after the 2016 Copa América Centenario final, then returned. This time the tone is different. There is no unfinished argument, no missing trophy and no need to prove that his club greatness could survive the Argentina shirt.

He leaves having won the one competition that defined the national conversation around him, having captained Argentina back to the summit of world fútbol, and having taken the team to one final World Cup final at 39.

The No. 10 shirt will be worn again. The era attached to it is finished.
